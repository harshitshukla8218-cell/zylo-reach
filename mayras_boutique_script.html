PART 1 — OPENING THE CALL
1. Confirm the Business & Permission
HARSH
"Hi, good morning! Am I speaking with Mayra’s Boutique?"
PROSPECT (MAYRA / STAFF)
"Yes, this is Mayra's Boutique. How can I help you?"
HARSH
"Hi! My name is Harsh, and I’m calling from Zylo Digital. We help independent fashion and apparel businesses with website development, creative visuals, and digital advertising. I came across your boutique while researching local retailers in Los Angeles. Is now an okay time for a brief two-minute conversation?"
PROSPECT — IF YES
"Sure, go ahead."
HARSH (THE HOOK)
"Thank you, I appreciate that. I noticed your boutique on Western Avenue has great local styling, but it appears your standalone online store isn't currently listed or active. I wanted to ask: are you currently working on launching an online catalog, or is your main focus on in-store sales at the moment?"
💡 Pro Tip: Say it "appears not to be currently listed or active." Never claim their site is broken or assume nobody has tried to build it.
2. Reach the Decision-Maker
HARSH
"Would I be speaking with Mayra or the person who handles website and marketing decisions for the boutique, or is there someone else in charge of that?"
PROSPECT
"I’m Mayra / I’m the store owner." (Or: "The owner isn't in right now.")
HARSH (IF OWNER)
"Wonderful, glad I reached you directly!"
PART 2 — DISCOVER THEIR NEEDS
HARSH
"To give you a little context, I understand your boutique offers women’s fashion and styling. I thought there might be some interesting ways to showcase your pieces online across Los Angeles, but I’d love to understand your current priorities first. What are your main goals for the business over the next few months?"
PROSPECT
"We want more people through the door / We want an online store / We’re doing okay with walk-ins."
HARSH
"That’s helpful. How are most of your customers discovering Mayra’s Boutique right now—is it mainly walk-in foot traffic along Western Ave, word of mouth, Instagram, or another channel?"
PROSPECT
"Mostly local walk-ins, and some people from Instagram."
HARSH
"Got it. Are you satisfied with how your current social media and online presence translate into sales, or is there an area where you feel you're leaving money on the table?"
PROSPECT
"We post clothes when new shipments come in, but it’s hard to stay consistent, and we don't have a website to buy from."
HARSH
"Understood. Have you already got an agency or freelancer helping you with product photos and web development, or are you managing all of that yourself?"
HARSH — FOLLOW-UP
"If you could improve one single thing about your store’s online visibility right now, what would make the biggest difference to your bottom line?"
PART 3 — MATCH THE SERVICE TO THE NEED
A. If They Need an E-Commerce Website
PROSPECT
"We really need to get an online store set up so customers don't just have to come to the shop."
HARSH
"That sounds worth exploring. For an independent boutique like yours, a clean, mobile-optimized online store allows customers across LA to browse new arrivals, check sizing, and order directly for delivery or pickup.

Our team specializes in fast, high-converting e-commerce web development. We’d first want to see whether you need a streamlined product catalog or a full online store. Would you be open to a short 15-minute call with our team to explore the right approach?"
B. If They Need Social Media & Product Content
PROSPECT
"Our main issue is product photos and getting noticed on Instagram."
HARSH
"Absolutely. Boutique collections offer great creative possibilities: weekly arrivals, outfit pairings, styling guides, and seasonal promos. At Zylo Digital, we create AI-powered product photoshoots, high-converting short-form video reels, and targeted Meta ads that put your pieces in front of active fashion shoppers in LA.

I’ll make sure our team focuses specifically on that visual and ad requirement. Would a short discovery meeting this week work for you?"
PART 4 — PRICING AND BUDGET OBJECTIONS
If They Ask: “How much do you charge?”
PROSPECT
"How much would something like this cost? What are your rates?"
HARSH
"We have two first-month options depending on scope: Digital Growth at $600 and Premium Growth at $800. The exact deliverables depend on what you need.

After the first month, our ongoing marketing-only support is $300 per month, and a standalone website project is $300, subject to agreed terms.

Rather than recommend a package that doesn't fit, I’d suggest we take 15 minutes to understand your goals and then provide clear pricing before any work begins."
If They Say: “That’s too expensive / We don’t have budget.”
HARSH
"I completely understand, Mayra. I wouldn’t want you to invest in anything that doesn’t fit your business. May I ask—is the main concern the budget itself, or are you not yet sure which deliverables would justify the investment?

(If budget): Thanks for clarifying. We can explore a smaller, clearly defined service first, like a website-only project for $300. Would you prefer to explore a website project or focus on creative marketing first?"
PART 5 — COMMON OBJECTIONS
“Just send me some information.”
HARSH
"Of course! To make sure I send you something truly relevant to Mayra’s Boutique, would you prefer an overview of our boutique website designs, or examples of our AI product photo & video showcases?

(Once answered): Perfect. What’s the best email to send that to? I'll send that over today and follow up early next week after you’ve had a chance to review it. Fair enough?"
“Can you guarantee more sales?”
HARSH
"I wouldn’t want to promise a result we can’t guarantee. Our objective is to strengthen Mayra's Boutique's digital presence, elevate your product visuals, and build a clear path from local attention to customer purchases. Actual revenue depends on product pricing, ad spend, and seasonal market demand."
PART 6 — CLOSE & BOOK THE 15-MINUTE CALL
HARSH
"Based on what you’ve shared, I think the next useful step would be a short 15-minute discovery meeting with our team. We can review your priorities, show you 2 or 3 creative ideas tailored for Mayra’s Boutique, and provide a clear written scope with timeline and pricing.

Would you be available for a brief 15-minute call this Thursday or Friday?"
PROSPECT
"Thursday works."
HARSH
"Wonderful! Would 11:00 AM or 3:00 PM Pacific Time work better for your schedule? ... Perfect, 3:00 PM PST.

May I confirm the best email address to send the calendar invitation and brief agenda to?

(Confirm): Just to confirm, we have you scheduled for Thursday at 3:00 PM Pacific Time. I’ll send the meeting invite right now. Thank you so much for your time, Mayra. Speak soon!"
